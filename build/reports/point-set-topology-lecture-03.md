## Corrections raised
1. **Consequential (from known-defects): Definition of the open ball $B_\varepsilon(x)$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$.**
   - **As transcribed:** $B_\varepsilon(x) = \lbrace y \in \mathbb{R}^n : \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - x_i)^2 < \varepsilon\rbrace$, called "the open ball of radius $\varepsilon$".
   - **What is meant:** $B_\varepsilon(x) = \lbrace y \in \mathbb{R}^n : \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - x_i)^2 < \varepsilon^2\rbrace$ (or equivalently, Euclidean distance $\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - x_i)^2} < \varepsilon$).
   - **Why:** The sum $\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - x_i)^2$ is the square of the Euclidean distance $\lVert y - x \rVert_2^2$. For the ball to have radius $\varepsilon$, the squared distance must be bounded by $\varepsilon^2$.
   - **Consequence / Invariance:** As $\varepsilon$ ranges over all positive reals $(0,\infty)$, the family of sets $\lbrace y \in \mathbb{R}^n : \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - x_i)^2 < \varepsilon\rbrace$ and the family $\lbrace y \in \mathbb{R}^n : \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - x_i)^2 < \varepsilon^2\rbrace$ are identical (by reparameterizing $\varepsilon' = \sqrt{\varepsilon}$). Therefore, the topology $\tau'$ generated by these balls and the equivalence $\tau = \tau'$ in Exercise 3.3 are completely unaffected. Both the defect and this invariance are explicitly stated in a Correction Note and accompanying supplement.
2. **Defect avoided / handled: Basis definition has one condition, not two.**
   - The lecturer begins: *"a subcollection $\mathcal{B}$ of $\tau$ is called a basis for $\tau$ if the following two conditions hold... I mean if the following condition holds..."*. He immediately self-corrected to a single condition: for every $U \in \tau$ and every $x \in U$, there is $W \in \mathcal{B}$ such that $x \in W \subseteq U$. We preserve the single defining condition without inventing a phantom second condition.
