### Where the library runs out

**Lectures 21, 22, 27, 28 and 34** — the path connectedness of
$GL_n(\mathbb{R})^+$ and $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$, the connectedness of $SO(n)$ and
$U(n)$, and the Grassmannian construction — are **not in Munkres, Simmons,
Mendelson, Kumaresan, Chinn–Steenrod, or Starbird–Su**. Morris's Appendix 5
(topological groups) is the only coverage in the library, and it is partial.

This matters for one reason. On those lectures, "check it against Munkres" will
come back empty, and **that is not evidence the lecturer is wrong.** Do not
raise a Correction Note because a result cannot be found. Verify the
mathematics on its own terms and say in the report that the library has no
parallel treatment.

### Where each book earns its place

**Munkres** — the default authority for every definition and statement, and the
book to point the reader toward for further reading, since he will meet it
again everywhere. Covers roughly 34 of the 40 lectures.

**Morris** — the second voice, and the *only* source for topological groups.
Free, actively maintained, generous with worked examples. Good when Munkres is
too terse. His "finite-closed topology" is the lecturer's finite-complement
topology; note the name difference when citing.

**Kumaresan** — closest to this lecturer's idiom and notation, and metric-space
dense. Reach for it first when you need a second worked example in the same
style, or when a step seems to assume a metric fact the lecture never proved.
Most valuable across L14–L15 and L28–L29.

**Simmons** — analysis-flavoured motivation. Best on "why define it this way",
and strong in the back half: compactness, normality, Urysohn.

**Mendelson** — the gentlest. Use it to calibrate a supplement's level: if
Mendelson spends a page on something, the reader needs that page. Also the
source for the elementary set theory the lectures use silently — De Morgan for
arbitrary families, used twice in L1 and never stated.

**Starbird–Su** — inquiry-based, mostly questions. Your exercise mine for
Parts I–III. Restate in your own words and the lecturer's notation; never copy
its numbering. Falls away after roughly L20.

**Chinn–Steenrod** — geometric and intuitive, with pictures. Use it to decide
*what a figure should show*. Not a source for point-set definitions. Falls away
after Part I.
